I stopped writing the same report five different ways, so I built something that does it for me.

Why every business tool gives everyone the same dashboard — and why that's the actual problem.

Swapnil S.Aug 22, 2026 · 6 min read

Every Monday for about a year, I rebuilt the same Q3 slide four times. Once for the CFO, who wanted the risk. Once for the CEO, who wanted the opportunity. Once for the board, who wanted neither and just wanted to know if it was safe. Same spreadsheet, every time. Just a different sentence at the top.

That's not a dashboard problem. Dashboards are fine at showing numbers. The problem is that a chart doesn't know who's looking at it, and a room full of executives never wants the same read on the same data.

"We're not shipping a dashboard. We're shipping the fifteen minutes back to a founder who used to spend a weekend building this deck by hand."

So Nixara starts before the chart. You upload a spreadsheet, or connect a live Tableau view, and ask a plain question — no query language, no pivot tables. It reads your role along with your question, and writes the report for the seat you're sitting in.

How it actually works

  • Upload — CSV, Excel, or a live Tableau connection. Handles messy data automatically.
  • Ask — plain language, your role, your time horizon.
  • Decide — three reports in under 60 seconds. Approve, reject, or postpone — then track what happened.

The part everyone skips

A forecast nobody checks is just a guess with better formatting. So every recommendation becomes a logged decision with an id and a timestamp, and months later Nixara compares what it predicted against what actually happened.
